# What the model leaves out

The tool compares costs. A build-or-buy decision also rests on things that are not costs, and on costs that are hard to put a number on. Read the result next to this list.

## Not in the model

- **Fit.** How well each option does the job. If off-the-shelf covers most of the process and the gap costs nothing, the cost comparison is the whole answer. If the gap is the part of the process that sets the business apart, it is not.
- **Time to start.** Off-the-shelf can be in use within days; custom takes the build. The bridge counts the money, not the value of starting early.
- **Risk.** A build can overrun; a vendor can raise prices, be sold, or retire the product. Put a range on the inputs you are unsure of and look at the sensitivity table instead of one answer.
- **Exit cost.** Leaving an off-the-shelf product means moving the data out and retraining people. Leaving custom software means finding someone who can maintain it. Neither is counted unless you add it as a one-time cost.
- **Tax and accounting.** A build may be capitalized and depreciated; a subscription is usually an operating expense. The tool works with cash, before tax.
- **Your own staff time on the project.** Time spent on requirements, reviews and testing, for either option, is not counted unless you add it.

## Simplifications

- Prices rise by a constant share each year. Real renewals move in steps.
- Users grow by a constant share each year and are counted at the start of the year.
- Support for custom software is flat each year. Older systems usually need more.
- The bridge during the build uses the off-the-shelf running cost as the cost of what the business uses today.

When one of these matters, change the scenario to reflect it, or run the scenario with several values and compare.
